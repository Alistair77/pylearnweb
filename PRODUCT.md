# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

A mixed audience, beginners first. Complete beginners who have never coded and need proof that Python is learnable plus an obvious first step; career switchers who want job-ready outcomes and credible, mentor-backed feedback; and students who want practice, projects and help decoding errors. They arrive from search or a recommendation, skim on a phone or laptop, and decide within a minute whether this feels achievable and legitimate.

## Product Purpose

PyLearnWeb is the marketing site for a Python learning platform: structured, project-based lessons plus real code review. The site's job is to make learning Python feel achievable and practical, explain the method (curriculum + feedback), present the three tracks, and move visitors to start a track or get in touch. Success is a visitor who understands what they would build and clicks into a track or the contact form.

## Brand Personality

Practical, confident, friendly. The voice of a calm senior developer explaining things plainly over your shoulder: specific, encouraging, never hypey. The Python REPL is the brand's native material (`>>>` prompts, `# comments`, typed output), used as a consistent system rather than decoration. Visitors should feel "I can actually do this" and "these people know what they're doing".

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS templates: centered hero stacks, identical icon + heading + text card grids, stock gradients, hero-metric blocks.
- Neon hacker cliché: green-on-black matrix code, glitch effects, "1337" costume. The terminal motif stays warm, readable and on-palette.
- Childish gamification (mascots, confetti, streak badges) is also off-voice for career switchers.

## Design Principles

1. **Show real code, not claims.** Wherever possible, demonstrate with actual Python and actual output instead of adjectives.
2. **Beginner-first, never dumbed down.** Plain language and obvious next steps, with enough substance that a career switcher trusts it.
3. **One motif, used consistently.** The REPL / comment / typed-output language is the brand system; every section speaks it the same way.
4. **Calm confidence over hype.** No inflated stats, no exclamation-mark marketing, no fake urgency.
5. **Fast and smooth is part of the brand.** A coding school with a janky site undercuts itself; motion must stay at 60fps and respect reduced motion.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: text contrast 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text) in both light and dark themes, visible keyboard focus on every interactive element, 44px touch targets on mobile, and full `prefers-reduced-motion` support (typing shows instantly, carets stop blinking, ambient canvases stay still). Content must never depend on color alone.
